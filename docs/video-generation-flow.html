Video Generator: flow, models, and libraries

This page documents the three generation modes, local narration voices, animated visuals, and what the app does after you submit a job.

No hosted AI API is called. Piper runs locally for speech. Stick-figure scenes are drawn with Pillow and require no image-generation model. Piper voice weights download from Hugging Face on first use.

Models and libraries

ComponentTaskExecution and license notes
Piper TTS: LJSpeech High (English female), Norman Medium (English male), Priyamvada Medium (Hindi female), and Pratham Medium (Hindi male) Converts the transcript, without translation, to a WAV narration file. Natural, calm, and upbeat presets change phoneme length/speaking pace only; they do not add emotional acting. Runs locally through ONNX Runtime. The English LJSpeech and Norman datasets are public domain. The Hindi Priyamvada and Pratham model cards specify CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; those voices are for non-commercial use only. Check current model cards and conditions before publishing.
Pillow Transcript-only mode draws animated multi-frame GIF scenes, including backgrounds, facial expressions, moving limbs, motion, and keyword-selected props/effects. Runs locally; no visual-generation model is downloaded and no transcript is sent to a hosted AI service. Scenes are illustrative rather than a full semantic translation of the narration.
Flask Web form, upload validation, background jobs, status and result endpoints. Python web framework; timestamped job logs and status are saved under output/<job-id>/job.json so completed or partial results can be recovered after a restart.
Browser Canvas + XMLHttpRequest Resizes still images before upload and reports progress. Videos are streamed to Flask in sequential 8 MB chunks, with a configurable total file-size limit. Runs in the browser; does not call an AI service. Chunking keeps each request small but does not increase the host's available disk space.
FFmpeg + ffprobe Fits or crops visuals, reads audio duration, encodes H.264/AAC MP4, and creates the looping GIF. FFmpeg errors are included in the job log. Runs locally on the server.

OpenAI, Claude, and Gemini APIs are not used by this project. The app does not send transcript text to a hosted AI provider and does not require an API key.

What happens after Generate

  1. The browser optimizes selected still images using Fit or Fill. Video files are split into sequential 8 MB chunks and assembled by Flask before generation.
  2. Flask validates the transcript and settings, stores the media, creates a background job, and returns its ID.
  3. The page polls the job status and displays timestamped server-side log entries; status/logs are also saved to disk for recovery.
  4. Piper synthesizes the narration. The app reads its duration with ffprobe.
  5. The selected visual mode animates stick-figure scenes or normalizes uploaded media; FFmpeg fits visuals to the narration duration and selected profile.
  6. FFmpeg writes an MP4 and a looping GIF preview (6 fps, 480 pixels wide). The page shows previews and download links.

Hosted deployment

The repository includes a Dockerfile and Render Blueprint. Use Deploy on Render to create a hosted service; Render provides its URL after the first build. The Blueprint uses a free instance and caps each video at 512 MB. Uploads are chunked at 8 MB per request, but free-instance storage is temporary and limited. Use a paid instance with a persistent disk for durable outputs or larger workloads, and set DATA_DIR to the disk mount path. The public app has no user login; anyone with its URL can submit jobs.
